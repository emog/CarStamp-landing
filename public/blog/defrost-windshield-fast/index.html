How to Defrost a Windshield Fast Without Cracking It

It is 7:40, you have to leave at 7:45, and the windshield is white. Here is the fastest way to clear it that won't crack the glass, wreck the wipers or leave you driving through a peephole.

The short answer

Start the engine, set the air to the windshield, the temperature to warm, the fan high, turn the A/C on and recirculation off, and switch on the rear window and mirror heaters. While that works, clear the snow and spray a de-icer on the glass, then scrape with a plastic scraper from the top down. Never pour hot water on the glass, and never drive until every window, the mirrors and the lights are clear. Most mornings that takes about five minutes.

Which heater settings defrost a windshield fastest?

  • Air to the windshield. Use the defrost button (a curved window with wavy arrows) if your car has one: many cars then turn on the A/C, open fresh air and run the fan high on their own.
  • A/C on. It sounds odd in winter, but the A/C dries the air, and dry air clears fog and frost from the inside of the glass far faster. It works with the heat on. In hard frost many cars won't run the A/C compressor at all, but leaving it on does no harm, and it starts drying the air as soon as it can.
  • Recirculation off. Cold outside air is drier than the damp air inside the car from your breath and wet shoes. Recirculating keeps the moisture in.
  • Warm, not blasting hot at first. The heater gives warm air only once the engine warms up, so the fan high on cool air is fine for the first minute. If the windshield has a chip or a crack, keep it cool for longer: a sudden warm stream on cold glass can make a chip spread.
  • Rear window, mirrors and heated windshield. Switch on every heater the car has. Some cars have a heated windshield with fine wires in the glass, which clears it in a couple of minutes.
  • Electric cars: warm the car up from the app while it is still plugged in, so the energy comes from the charger, not the battery. Many let you schedule it for the time you leave.

How to scrape ice without scratching the glass

  • Use a plastic scraper, ideally with a brush on the other end. Never metal, a key or a spatula: they scratch the glass, and scratches flare in oncoming headlights.
  • Brush the snow off first, including the roof and the hood. Snow from the roof slides onto the windshield when you brake, and in some places, including the UK and several U.S. states, driving with it can get you fined.
  • Scrape from the top down, with short strokes at a low angle, and let the defroster soften the ice while you work.
  • On the rear window, let the heater do the work. If you must scrape, go gently along the heating lines, never across them, so the blade doesn't catch and cut them.
  • Don't scrape tinted film on the inside of side windows: clear them with heat.

De-icer: shop-bought or homemade

A de-icer spray melts the ice in a minute or two and makes scraping much easier. Shop-bought ones are alcohol-based and work down to well below freezing.

You can mix one at home: two parts isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol, the common 70% kind, to one part water in a spray bottle, with a drop of dish soap if you like. It stays liquid far below freezing, so you can keep it in the car. Alcohol is flammable: keep it away from flames and out of children's reach. Spray it on, wait a minute and wipe or scrape the slush away.

Salt water also melts ice, but it is hard on paint, rubber seals and wiper blades. Leave it for the driveway.

What never to do

  • Hot or boiling water. A sudden heat on frozen glass can crack it, especially from a small chip. Even lukewarm water often refreezes on the glass, the wipers and the ground under your feet.
  • Wipers on frozen glass. Frozen blades tear their rubber edges, and the motor can burn out. Free them by hand once the glass has softened.
  • Driving through a peephole. Clear the whole windshield, the side windows, the mirrors and the lights. Pedestrians and cyclists come from the sides.
  • Leaving the engine running unattended. Cars are stolen this way every winter, insurance may not pay, and in some countries, including the UK, it is against the law on a public road. Stay with the car.
  • Idling with the exhaust buried in snow. Clear around the tailpipe first: a blocked exhaust can fill the car with carbon monoxide.

Why the windshield fogs up on the inside, and how to stop it

Fog and frost on the inside are moisture in the car, not the weather outside. To get rid of it:

  • Use the A/C with fresh air, as above, every time you drive in the damp months.
  • Clean the inside of the glass. Dirt and the film from the dashboard plastics give moisture something to cling to. A glass cleaner and a microfiber cloth make a big difference.
  • Dry the floor mats. Wet mats from snowy shoes keep the car damp for days. Take rubber mats inside to dry, or use a dehumidifier bag (silica gel) on the dashboard.
  • Check the cabin filter. A clogged pollen filter cuts the airflow and slows defrosting. It is usually changed once a year, at the service.
  • Look for a leak. If the carpet is wet with no snow brought in, a door or windshield seal or a blocked drain may be letting water in.

How to stop the windshield freezing overnight

  • Cover the windshield overnight with a windshield cover, an old towel or a piece of cardboard, held by the doors or the wipers.
  • Lift the wipers off the glass on frosty nights, or park with them in the service position some cars have, so they don't freeze to it.
  • Park under cover if you can: a carport, a tree (outside of storm season) or next to a wall cuts frost a lot.
  • Use a winter screenwash rated for your coldest mornings. Water freezes in the reservoir and on the glass. The winter car check covers the other winter fluids.
  • Frozen doors: rub the rubber seals with a silicone spray or a little petroleum jelly in autumn. For a frozen lock, use a lock de-icer, never hot water.

The five-minute routine

  1. Start the engine; check the exhaust is clear of snow.
  2. Air to the windshield, fan high, A/C on, recirculation off; rear window and mirror heaters on.
  3. Brush the snow off the roof, the hood, the windows and the lights.
  4. Spray de-icer, wait a minute, scrape from the top down with a plastic scraper.
  5. Free the wipers by hand, clear the mirrors and side windows, then go.

Questions about defrosting a windshield

What is the fastest way to defrost a windshield?

Run the engine with the air on the windshield, the fan high, the A/C on and recirculation off, spray an alcohol-based de-icer on the outside, wait a minute and scrape from the top down with a plastic scraper.

Can hot water crack a windshield?

Yes. Pouring hot or boiling water on frozen glass heats it suddenly and unevenly, which can crack it, especially if there is already a chip. The water also refreezes on the glass and the ground.

Why should I use the A/C to defrost in winter?

The A/C dries the air before the heater warms it, and dry air clears fog and frost from the inside of the glass much faster. It works together with the heat, so the air stays warm.

Should you use hot or cold air to defrost a windshield?

Start with the fan high on whatever the heater gives, and let the air warm up as the engine does, with the A/C on and recirculation off. Warm, dry air clears the glass fastest, but a sudden blast of hot air on frozen glass with a chip in it can make the chip spread, so let it warm gradually.

How do I make homemade de-icer?

Mix two parts isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol, the common 70% kind, with one part water in a spray bottle. It stays liquid far below freezing, so it can be kept in the car and sprayed on the ice before scraping.

Why does my windshield fog up on the inside?

There is moisture in the car, from breath, wet shoes and floor mats, which condenses on the cold glass. Use the A/C with fresh air, keep the inside of the glass clean and dry out wet mats.

How do I stop my windshield freezing overnight?

Cover it with a windshield cover, an old towel or a piece of cardboard held by the doors or the wipers, lift the wipers off the glass, and park under cover or next to a wall if you can. A winter screenwash in the reservoir keeps the washers working on frosty mornings.
