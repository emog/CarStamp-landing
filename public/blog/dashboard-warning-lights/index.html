Car Dashboard Warning Lights: What They Mean and When to Stop

A light comes on halfway to work. Is it a pull-over-now problem or a book-the-garage problem? The color answers most of that, and the symbols are much the same from one car to the next.

The short answer

Dashboard warning lights are color-coded by urgency, much like traffic lights. Red means stop as soon as it is safe and switch the engine off, or at least don't drive far: oil pressure, engine temperature, brakes and charging are the ones that can wreck an engine or take away your brakes. Amber (yellow) means check soon: the car is usually safe to drive gently to a garage, but something needs attention. Green and blue are information: headlights, high beam, cruise control. A light that flashes is more urgent than the same light steady. When in doubt, your owner's manual has a page with every symbol your car uses.

Red warning lights: stop as soon as it is safe

Oil pressure light (an oil can)

The engine is not getting enough oil pressure, and without it the engine can be ruined in minutes. Pull over, switch off, wait a few minutes and check the level on the dipstick. If it is low, top up with the oil your manual names and watch whether the light goes out. If the level is right and the light stays on, don't drive: call for help. Some cars also have an amber oil level light, which is less urgent: top up soon.

Engine temperature light (a thermometer in waves)

The engine is overheating. Stop, switch off and let it cool for at least 30 minutes. Never open the coolant cap on a hot engine: the coolant is under pressure and can scald. Once it is cold, check the level in the reservoir. Low coolant usually means a leak, so even after a top-up, have it checked before a long drive. A blue thermometer on some cars only means the engine is still cold.

Brake warning light (a circle with an exclamation mark, or BRAKE)

First check that the parking brake is fully off. If it is, the brake fluid may be low, from a leak or very worn pads, or the brake system has a fault. If the pedal feels soft or goes lower than usual, don't drive on. If the light comes on together with the ABS light, have it checked before driving further.

Battery or charging light (a battery)

The battery is not being charged, usually because of the alternator or its belt. The car will run on the battery for a while, often less than an hour. Switch off what you don't need (heated seats, the rear window heater, the radio) and drive straight to a garage if one is close; don't switch the engine off before you get there, as it may not start again. On some engines the same belt drives the water pump, so if the temperature gauge starts to climb as well, stop.

Electric and hybrid cars

A red high-voltage or powertrain warning, or a turtle symbol with reduced power, means the system has limited itself to protect the battery or the motor. Pull over where it is safe and follow the manual; many of these faults need the car to be checked before it is driven again.

Airbag and power steering: important, but not stop-now

  • Airbag (a seated person with a circle in front): the airbags or seat belt tensioners may not work in a crash. Red on many cars, amber on others. The car drives normally, but book it in soon.
  • Power steering (a steering wheel with an exclamation mark): steering may get heavy, especially at low speed. Drive gently and have it checked; if steering suddenly becomes very hard, stop.
  • Seat belt, door or trunk open: they mean what they say. Fix them before you set off.

Amber (yellow) warning lights: get the car checked soon

Check engine light (an engine outline)

Steady, it means the engine computer has stored a fault: it could be anything from a loose fuel cap to a failing sensor. Drive normally and book the car in. Flashing is different: the engine is misfiring, and unburned fuel can overheat and destroy the catalytic converter. Slow down, avoid hard acceleration and towing, and get to a garage soon, or stop if the car runs badly.

ABS light

The anti-lock system is off, but the normal brakes still work. Brake more gently on wet or icy roads, since the wheels can lock, and have it checked.

Traction or stability control (a car with wavy lines)

When it flashes for a moment on a slippery road, the system is working: ease off the throttle. When it stays on, either the system has been switched off (look for an OFF symbol or a button) or it has a fault and won't help you on ice.

Tire pressure light (a horseshoe with an exclamation mark)

One or more tires is low. Check all four with a gauge, cold if you can, and fill them to the figures on the door jamb label. A light that comes on on a cold morning and goes off as you drive means the pressures are borderline: top them up. If it flashes for a minute and then stays on, the monitoring system itself has a fault. Why the cold does this, and how much the pressure drops, is in tire pressure in cold weather.

Diesel: glow plugs, DPF and AdBlue

  • Glow plugs (a coil): wait for it to go out before you start the engine, especially in the cold. If it flashes while you drive, on many cars it reports an engine fault.
  • DPF (a box with dots): the diesel particulate filter is filling up, usually from too many short trips. Many manuals ask for 10 to 20 minutes at a steady speed over about 40 mph (65 km/h) so it can clean itself. If the light stays on after that, see a garage before the filter blocks.
  • AdBlue: the tank is low. Top it up soon: many cars count down the miles left and then refuse to restart once it is empty.

Brake pads, service and the rest

  • Brake pad wear (a circle with dashes at the sides): the pads are near their limit. Book a brake check soon.
  • Service due (a wrench): the car's service interval is up. Not a fault, but don't put it off for months.
  • Low fuel: most cars have some 30 to 50 miles (50 to 80 km) left, but it varies. Running a modern car dry is hard on the fuel pump, and a diesel may need its fuel system bled.
  • Frost (a snowflake): the outside temperature is near freezing, around 37 °F (3 °C). Expect ice, especially on bridges and in shade, and see how to defrost a windshield fast for frosty mornings.
  • Washer fluid low: top up, with a winter mix in the cold. The winter car check covers the other fluids.

Green and blue lights: just information

Green lights show that something is on: dipped headlights, front fog lights, turn signals, cruise control, an eco or ready mode. Blue is the high beam. Rear fog lights are usually amber. None of these is a fault, but a high beam left on dazzles oncoming drivers, and fog lights left on in clear weather do too.

What to do when a warning light comes on

  1. Note the color and whether it is steady or flashing.
  2. If it is red, find a safe place to stop, switch off and look it up in the manual.
  3. Notice anything else: a smell, a noise, a change in how the car drives or brakes, a gauge moving.
  4. Write down the date and the mileage. A garage finds a fault faster when you can say when it started and what you noticed.
  5. Ask for the fault codes. Every car sold in the U.S. since 1996 and in Europe since about 2001 (petrol) and 2004 (diesel) has a diagnostic port under the dashboard. Cheap code readers plug into it, and many parts stores read codes free. A code points to where the fault is; it is not the whole diagnosis.

A tip when buying a used car

When you turn the key or press Start, almost every warning light comes on for a second or two as a self-test, then goes out. If a light such as the airbag or check engine light never comes on at all, its bulb may have been removed to hide a fault. Watch the dashboard at start-up on any car you are about to buy.

Questions about dashboard warning lights

Can I drive with the check engine light on?

If it is steady and the car drives normally, yes, gently, to a garage soon. If it is flashing, the engine is misfiring: slow down, avoid hard acceleration and get it checked right away, or stop if the car runs badly.

What is the difference between red and amber warning lights?

Red means a problem that can damage the car or affect safety right away, so stop as soon as it is safe. Amber means something needs checking soon, but the car can usually be driven gently to a garage.

Which dashboard warning lights are the most serious?

The red ones: oil pressure, engine temperature, brakes and charging, along with a flashing check engine light. Each can damage the engine or affect braking within minutes, so stop as soon as it is safe; with the charging light, drive only as far as the nearest garage.

Can I drive with the battery light on?

Only a short way. The battery is not being charged, and the car runs on what is left in it, often for less than an hour. Switch off what you don't need, drive straight to a garage without stopping the engine, and stop if the temperature gauge starts to climb as well.

Why did a warning light come on and then go off?

Some faults come and go, such as a sensor near its limit or tire pressures that are low only when cold. The car usually stores a fault code even after the light goes out, so mention it at the next service.

Will a warning light go off after the fault is fixed?

Many go out on their own once the cause is fixed, sometimes after a few drives. Others, such as some check engine and service lights, need a garage or a code reader to clear them.

What does it mean when all the lights come on at start-up?

That is normal: the car tests its warning lights for a second or two when you switch on. A light that stays on after the engine starts is the one to look at.
