Tire Pressure in Cold Weather: How Much It Drops and What to Do

The first frosty morning of the year, and the tire pressure light is on. Nothing is punctured: the air inside your tires has simply shrunk with the cold. Here is how much it drops, why it matters, and how to set the pressure right for winter.

The short answer

A tire pressure light on a cold morning usually means the cold has lowered the pressure, not that a tire is punctured. Tire pressure drops about 1 psi for every 10 °F the air cools, roughly 0.1 bar for every 8 °C. Tires set in a warm garage or in summer can be 4 to 6 psi (0.3 to 0.4 bar) low on a cold morning. Check them cold, before driving, at least once a month and after every big drop in temperature, and fill them to the figures on the label in the driver's door jamb, not the number on the tire.

Why does tire pressure drop in cold weather?

Air contracts as it cools, so the same amount of air in a tire pushes out less. Tires also lose a little air through the rubber all the time, about 1 to 2 psi (0.07 to 0.14 bar) a month, and cold rims and valves can make small leaks worse. In autumn the two add up: pressures set in September can be well down by the first frost.

A worked example: you set 35 psi in a 68 °F garage. The next morning it is 18 °F outside. That is 50 °F colder, so the tires read about 30 psi, some 14% under. In metric: 2.4 bar at 20 °C becomes about 2.05 bar at -8 °C.

Nitrogen doesn't change this. It leaks out a little more slowly than air, but it reacts to cold the same way.

Why a few psi matter

  • Grip and braking: an underinflated tire flexes more, its tread doesn't sit flat, and it grips worse on wet and snowy roads, just when you need it most. Tread depth and winter tire rules are in the winter car check.
  • Wear: low pressure wears the outer edges (the shoulders) of the tread faster.
  • Fuel and range: soft tires roll harder, which costs fuel, or range in an EV, which loses range in the cold already.
  • Heat: a tire that flexes too much heats up on the highway, and that is how tires fail.

Why the tire pressure light comes on when it's cold, then goes off

The tire pressure light (a horseshoe shape with an exclamation mark) comes on when a tire falls well below its set pressure. In the U.S. it must come on by the time a tire is 25% under the recommended pressure; in Europe, at about 20%. On a cold morning a borderline tire crosses that line. As you drive, the tires warm up, the pressure rises by 2 to 4 psi (0.15 to 0.3 bar), and the light goes off.

That doesn't mean all is well: the tires are still low in the cold, and you only see it at the coldest time of day. Top them up. The other lights on the dashboard, and which ones mean stop, are in dashboard warning lights.

Two more things to know:

  • Direct systems have a sensor in each wheel and often show each tire's pressure. Indirect systems work out low pressure from wheel speed and need a reset in the car's settings after you adjust the pressures or swap tires. Your manual says which you have.
  • A light that flashes for about a minute and then stays on usually means the system itself has a fault, often a dead sensor battery or a wheel without a sensor, such as a winter set.

How to check tire pressure in winter

  1. Check cold. The car should have stood for three hours or more, or been driven less than a mile or two. Morning, before you set off, is best.
  2. Check at the outside temperature. Pressure set in a heated garage drops when the car goes out. If you have to fill them inside, add about 1 psi for every 10 °F (0.1 bar for every 8 °C) the outside is colder than the garage.
  3. Use the door jamb label. It gives the pressures for your car, front and rear, and often a higher figure for a full load. The number on the tire's sidewall is the maximum it can take, not what to fill it to. Some manuals give a separate figure for winter tires, often 0.2 bar (3 psi) higher.
  4. Use your own gauge. Gauges at gas stations are often a few psi off. A good digital or dial gauge is cheap and reads the same every time.
  5. If you fill up warm, for example at a station after a drive, fill to about 3 psi (0.2 bar) above the label figure, then check again cold the next morning.
  6. Never let air out of warm tires. The rise when driving is normal and already allowed for.
  7. Check the spare, if you have one. It goes down too, and you only find out when you need it.
  8. Put the valve caps back. They keep out the dirt and salty water that make valves leak.

Why does one tire keep losing air?

If one tire needs topping up far more often than the others, it is not the cold. Common causes are a nail or screw in the tread, a valve that leaks, or corrosion where the tire seals on an alloy rim, which road salt makes worse. A tire shop can find it with soapy water in a few minutes. Get it fixed before a long trip.

The winter tire pressure routine

  1. Check all four tires and the spare in the first cold week of autumn.
  2. Then check once a month, and after any sudden cold snap.
  3. Always check cold, at the outside temperature, with your own gauge.
  4. Fill to the door jamb figures, the loaded figure before a full trip.
  5. Reset an indirect monitoring system after you adjust the pressures.
  6. Check again in spring: pressure set in January rises as it warms up.

Questions about tire pressure in winter

How much does tire pressure drop in cold weather?

About 1 psi for every 10 °F the air cools, roughly 0.1 bar for every 8 °C. A tire set in a warm garage can be 4 to 6 psi (0.3 to 0.4 bar) low on a frosty morning.

What should my tire pressure be in winter?

The figure on the label in the driver's door jamb, or in the owner's manual, checked cold at the outside temperature. Filling to that figure in the cold already puts more air in than in summer. Some manuals give a slightly higher figure for winter tires, often about 3 psi (0.2 bar); never go above the maximum on the sidewall.

Is it safe to drive with the tire pressure light on?

A short, gentle drive to an air pump is usually fine if no tire looks flat and the car steers normally. Check all four tires with a gauge as soon as you can and fill them to the door jamb figures. If one tire is much lower than the others, or the light comes back within days, have that tire checked for a leak.

How often should I check tire pressure in winter?

At least once a month and after every sudden cold snap, always cold, before driving. Tires lose about 1 to 2 psi a month on their own, and every 10 °F drop in temperature takes about another 1 psi (0.1 bar for every 8 °C).

Why does my tire pressure light come on in the morning and go off later?

On a cold morning a slightly low tire falls below the warning level. As you drive, the tires warm up and the pressure rises, so the light goes out. The tires are still low when cold, so top them up.

Is it normal for tire pressure to go up when driving?

Yes. Tires warm up as you drive, and the pressure rises by about 2 to 4 psi (0.15 to 0.3 bar). That is allowed for in the recommended figures, so never let air out of warm tires.

Does nitrogen stop tire pressure dropping in the cold?

No. Nitrogen leaks through the rubber a little more slowly than air, but it shrinks in the cold the same way, so the pressure still drops.
